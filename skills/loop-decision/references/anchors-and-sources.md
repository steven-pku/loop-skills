# Anchors and Sources

Every framework this skill uses, with its exact public citation and evidence tier. Tiers: **primary** (original text read) / **consensus** (multi-source convergent, original not directly read) / **rule of thumb** (single source or inference — not used as an anchor here). All URLs as accessed 2026-07-12.

## Premortem

- **Primary**: Klein, Gary. "Performing a Project Premortem." *Harvard Business Review*, September 2007, pp. 18-19 (Reprint F0709A). https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem
- Method first appeared in book form in Klein, *The Power of Intuition* (Doubleday, 2004). Klein dates the method's invention to ~1990-91 (Psychology Today, 2021-01: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/seeing-what-others-dont/202101/the-pre-mortem-method ).
- **Kahneman's endorsement** — two verifiable venues: *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011), Part 3 ch. 24 ("the premortem's main virtue: it legitimizes doubts", consensus tier via multiple independent reading notes); McKinsey Quarterly interview, 2010-03 ("Strategic decisions: When can you trust your gut?", primary: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/strategic-decisions-when-can-you-trust-your-gut ).
- ⚠️ **Known misquote we do not repeat**: the widely circulated Kahneman line "the single most valuable decision-making technique I know" has no traceable primary source. Not used here.

## The "30%" figure — corrected reading

- **Actual study**: Mitchell, D. J., Russo, J. E., & Pennington, N. (1989). "Back to the future: Temporal perspective in the explanation of events." *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*, 2(1), 25-38. doi:10.1002/bdm.3960020103
- What it found: imagining an outcome **as certain** (prospective hindsight) increased the **number of reasons generated** by ~30%; the study did not evaluate reason accuracy or quality (convergent reading: Jason Collins, https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/premortem ; Russo & Schoemaker, *Winning Decisions*, 2002, report a sister experiment at ~25%).
- Klein's HBR phrasing ("increases the ability to correctly identify reasons... by 30%") is a strengthened restatement. This skill cites the study for **reason generation volume**, nothing more.

## Direct experimental evidence for premortem

- **Primary**: Veinott, Klein & Wiggins (2010). "Evaluating the Effectiveness of the PreMortem Technique on Plan Confidence." *7th ISCRAM Conference*. Premortem reduced overconfidence vs. pro/con and cons-only conditions (n=178). PDF: https://idl.iscram.org/files/veinott/2010/1049_Veinott_etal2010.pdf
- **Consensus**: Gallop & Bischoff (2016). "How to catch a black swan: Measuring the benefits of the premortem technique for risk identification." *Journal of Enterprise Transformation*, 6(2), 87-106. doi:10.1080/19488289.2016.1240118

## Type 1 / Type 2 decisions (one-way vs two-way doors)

- **Primary**: Jeff Bezos, *2015 Letter to Shareholders* (Amazon, published April 2016 — the letter titles itself "2015", often miscited as 2016), "Invention Machine" section. PDF: https://s2.q4cdn.com/299287126/files/doc_financials/annual/2015-Letter-to-Shareholders.PDF
- Both directions matter: the letter warns against one-size-fits-all heavy process on Type 2 decisions; its footnote notes the inverse failure (running Type 1 decisions through lightweight process) is punished "before extinction" — survivorship applies. The gate in this skill calibrates both ways.

## Decision journal / memo field lineage

- **Primary**: Farnam Street, "The Decision Journal" — 8 core fields (situation / problem statement / variables / complexities / alternatives / ranges of outcomes / expected outcome with probabilities / time & state). https://fs.blog/decision-journal/
- **Primary**: SPADE (Gokul Rajaram, Square): Setting / People / Alternatives / Decide / Explain. https://docs.superhuman.com/@gokulrajaram/gokuls-spade-toolkit ; First Round Review write-up: https://review.firstround.com/square-defangs-difficult-decisions-with-this-system-heres-how/
- **Primary**: DACI (Atlassian Team Playbook): Driver / Approver (exactly one) / Contributors / Informed. https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci
- **Primary**: ADR (Michael Nygard, 2011): Title / Status / Context / Decision / Consequences — the Status lifecycle this skill's memo borrows. https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions
- **Consensus**: Amazon 6-pager narrative memo culture (Bryar & Carr, *Working Backwards*, 2021) — cited only for the mechanism "narrative writing forces precise thinking"; it is a document culture, not a field template.

## Process vs outcome ("resulting")

- The term "resulting" for judging decisions by outcomes is popularized in poker/decision literature (Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets*, 2018 — consensus tier). The outcome-blind retrospective order in this skill is a structural guard, not a reading recommendation.
